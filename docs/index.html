Scoring approach

Threshold-based indicators

For indicators with a defined threshold (e.g. yield curve at 0, Sahm Rule at 0.5), risk is computed as the signed distance from the threshold, passed through a logistic function: 1 / (1 + e^(−4x)). Crossing the threshold gives a score of 50.

Z-score indicators

For indicators without a fixed threshold (e.g. unemployment, GDP), a z-score is computed over a rolling 36-month window. The z-score is then passed through the same logistic function. Mean = 50, ±1σ ≈ 73 and 27.

Alert states

Composite score 0–29: GREEN (low risk). 30–59: YELLOW (elevated watch). 60–100: RED (high risk). Thresholds reflect judgment, not backtested calibration.

History backfill

On each data refresh, the full 24-month history is recomputed by replaying monthly snapshots against the FRED series, using only data available at each cutoff date (real-time z-score windows). No look-ahead bias.

Limitations: Layer weights are doctrinal, not empirically optimized against NBER chronology. Alert thresholds are calibrated by intuition. Series have different release lags (daily ≈ 1 day stale, monthly ≈ 1–2 months, quarterly ≈ 3–4 months). The composite blends these without stale-adjusting. No backtest has been run. Treat as relative signal monitoring, not a recession prediction model.
